What may be changing?

Exhale-emphasised slow breathing

May influence respiratory rate and cardiac autonomic regulation. Individual comfort matters; paced breathing should not become forced over-breathing.

PROMISING · SOURCED
Affect labelling

Putting feelings into words has been studied alongside changes in emotion-related neural responses. STATE does not promise a fixed percentage reduction or a universal 90-second rule.

MECHANISM EVIDENCE

Source examples: PubMed 36630953 · PubMed 38063977 · Psychological Science affect-labelling research. Full production cards require reviewer sign-off.

Where did the practice come from?

Breath regulation

Prāṇāyāma belongs to Indian yogic traditions with many distinct methods and aims. STATE must name the specific practice and avoid presenting an ancient tradition as a modern neuroscience hack.

LINEAGE REQUIRED
Mindfulness and loving-kindness

Buddhist contemplative traditions distinguish attention, insight and mettā practices. Their ethical and liberative contexts should remain visible.

CONTEXT REQUIRED

Canonical references can include Yoga Sūtras, early Buddhist discourses, contemplative Christian texts and other primary sources—reviewed with practitioners from those traditions.

What did STATE measure?

  • Your self-rated intensity before and after
  • Completion and observable action
  • Duration and time of day
  • Optional heart rate, HRV, breath rate or steps when connected
  • The next-hour question

Personal patterns are hypotheses about you—not proof of causality.

What did we not measure?

  • STATE did not measure dopamine, cortisol or norepinephrine.
  • A phone cannot tell you that an amygdala “switched off”.
  • Brain regions work in networks; colourful diagrams are explanatory models.
  • These practices do not replace medical, psychological or crisis care.
