# STATE V6 — Source Standard

STATE may be playful, poetic and spiritually open. Its claims must remain traceable.

## Three separate evidence lanes

### 1. Scientific evidence
Every empirical claim records:

- exact claim text;
- population and context;
- source type: primary study, systematic review, guideline or expert consensus;
- direct source link or DOI;
- evidence strength;
- known limitations and plausible harms;
- reviewer, review date and next review date.

A brain image is never presented as a live scan of the user. Network-level language is preferred over claims that one brain region simply switches on or off.

### 2. Wisdom and contemplative tradition
Every practice derived from a tradition records:

- the named tradition and practice;
- a canonical or historical source where possible;
- the ethical, communal or liberative context that surrounded it;
- the modern adaptation STATE made;
- a qualified practitioner or tradition-informed reviewer;
- cultural and translation limitations.

Ancient practice must not be reduced to a neuroscience hack. A spiritual teaching is not converted into a medical claim merely because the user finds it meaningful.

### 3. Personal evidence
STATE may record:

- before-and-after self-report;
- observable action or completion;
- duration, time and context;
- optional wearable signals;
- next-hour outcome;
- qualitative notes.

These records can reveal personal patterns. They do not establish causality or general scientific efficacy.

## Claim states

1. **Draft** — not public.
2. **Sourced** — source attached, not yet reviewed.
3. **Reviewed** — evidence and wording approved.
4. **Published** — visible with source and limitations.
5. **Challenged** — concern or contrary evidence added.
6. **Revised or withdrawn** — public correction retained.

## Language rules

Prefer:

- “may influence”;
- “has been associated with”;
- “this study found in this population”;
- “STATE measured your self-rated intensity.”

Avoid:

- “switches off your amygdala”;
- “releases 250% more dopamine” without matching protocol and measurement;
- “works for everyone”;
- precise universal claims such as “emotion falls by 50% in 90 seconds” without adequate evidence.

## Publication gate

No formula enters the public evidence shelf until:

- its intended outcome is defined;
- the intervention and population are bounded;
- claims are linked to sources;
- limitations and contraindications are visible;
- science and tradition reviewers have approved their respective lanes;
- the product does not imply diagnosis or treatment unless legally and clinically supported.
