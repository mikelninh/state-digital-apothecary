# STATE Ownership Blueprint

## The ambition

A contributor should be able to say:

> I helped create this project. Here is the agreed mission, my accepted contribution, the contract that defines my rights and the proof that the work was delivered.

That is more meaningful than receiving a decorative token.

## Start with rights, not a DAO

The correct order is:

1. Define the project and legal entity.
2. Define contributions and acceptance criteria.
3. Define economic and governance rights in plain language.
4. Sign an enforceable agreement.
5. Record proof of accepted contribution.
6. Automate selected rights with smart contracts only where this improves trust.
7. Decentralise governance gradually after the process works with humans.

A blockchain record cannot create rights that the legal agreement does not recognise.

## Three ownership layers

### 1. Proof of contribution

A portable record containing:
- project and mission ID;
- contributor identity or privacy-preserving identifier;
- agreed deliverable;
- reviewer or acceptance authority;
- completion evidence;
- timestamp and version;
- dispute status.

The receipt may begin as signed JSON and later be anchored on-chain.

### 2. Project participation contract

Each project can offer one or more rights:
- fixed payment;
- revenue share from that formula or product;
- profit share after defined costs;
- voting rights on a bounded project domain;
- attribution and portfolio rights;
- licence or intellectual-property rights;
- cooperative membership.

The contract must define vesting, expiry, transferability, taxes, removal, disputes and what happens if the project closes.

### 3. Governance membership

Governance should not simply equal capital.

Possible chambers:
- contributors;
- users and beneficiaries;
- evidence and ethics reviewers;
- long-term stewards;
- capital providers with limited scope.

Important decisions may require approval from more than one chamber.

## Recommended progression

### Phase A — Digital proof, conventional contracts
- Signed contributor agreements.
- GitHub and evidence artefacts.
- Project ledger.
- Non-transferable contribution receipts.

### Phase B — Project-specific revenue participation
- Separate pool for one formula or mission.
- Clear revenue definition and payout waterfall.
- Dashboard showing accrued and paid amounts.
- Legal review before public sale or broad transferability.

### Phase C — Cooperative membership
- Contributors and stewards become members of an appropriate legal entity.
- One-person or chamber-balanced governance protects against plutocracy.
- Treasury decisions become participatory.

### Phase D — DAO-assisted governance
- On-chain proposals, treasury execution and proof anchoring.
- Legal wrapper remains responsible for contracts, employment, consumer rights and taxes.
- Emergency and dispute procedures exist outside code.

## What STATE must avoid

- selling “ownership” that is only symbolic;
- implying securities-like returns without legal review;
- pay-to-govern plutocracy;
- transferable reputation that creates a black market;
- permanent public identity exposure;
- governance theatre where founders retain hidden control;
- smart contracts with no human appeal process;
- token speculation becoming more important than useful formulas.

## First pilot

The safest first ownership experiment is one bounded formula:

**Project:** CALM 10 public beta  
**Mission:** research, design, build and test a ten-minute regulation ritual.  
**Budget:** fixed and public.  
**Roles:** researcher, product designer, facilitator, engineer, tester coordinator.  
**Rights:** fixed compensation plus a small project revenue pool for accepted contributors.  
**Proof:** signed contribution receipts linked to versioned artefacts.  
**Governance:** contributors vote only on formula-specific changes within a defined scope.  

This creates real participation without pretending the entire company is already a decentralised nation-state.
